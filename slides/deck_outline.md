**Speaker notes.** Here's the physics in one picture. Two mics at 470.50 and 471.25 megahertz. Their signals mix inside the electronics, and third-order products appear at two f1 minus f2 and two f2 minus f1: 469.75 and 472.00. If we put a third mic at 472.00, it hears that ghost. ## Slide 5: The bridge: planning rules become qubit interactions

- 1. Rules: Mics 500 kHz apart. No product within 250 kHz of a mic.
- 2. Conflict sets: 15 close pairs, 56 triples (2f_k − f_l), 252 quadruples (f_k + f_l − f_p)
- 3. Cost on 16 qubits: x_k = (1 − Z_k)/2 gives 948 Z-strings, up to 4 Z's each
- 4. QAOA: Dicke start and XY mixer keep exactly 5 mics in every sample
- [figure: figures/fig_cost_equation.png] C(x) equals the sum over close pairs of x_k x_l, plus the sum over triples of x_k x_l x_m, plus the sum over quadruples of x_k x_l x_p x_m, with the x values summing to 5 mics.
- A plan is IM-free exactly when its cost is 0
- Three- and four-way IM rules go in directly as 3- and 4-qubit Z terms
- The mic count is fixed by the circuit, so there is no penalty weight to tune

**Speaker notes.** Here's the bridge. Each allowed frequency is a qubit: 1 means a mic sits there. The rules become three kinds of conflict: close pairs, triples where a two-tone product lands on a third mic, and quadruples where a three-tone product lands on a fourth. Substituting x equals one minus Z over two gives a diagonal Hamiltonian with 948 Z-strings. QAOA starts in a Dicke state, every five-mic plan with equal weight, and the XY mixer only swaps a one and a zero, so it moves mics but never adds or removes one. No penalty weights. The circuit is built with Qiskit's qaoa_ansatz function.

## Slide 8: Why quantum, honestly

- No speed-up: 4,368. plans, all checked by brute force in about 10 ms. Annealing ends clean in 50 of 50 runs.
- A modelling fit: 16 vs 121. qubits here vs variables as a QUBO. No auxiliary variables, no count penalty.
- The price: 12,716. CNOTs at p = 3. Four-way terms cost circuit depth.
- We found no earlier quantum approach that models IM products.

**Speaker notes.** Why quantum? Three honest answers. First, there's no speed-up: brute force solves this in about 10 milliseconds, and simulated annealing ends clean in 50 of 50 runs. Second, a modelling fit: the IM rules are three- and four-way, and QAOA takes them as is on 16 qubits. A QUBO needs 121 variables after greedy Rosenberg substitution, 114 to 125 depending on tie-breaks, plus a penalty for the mic count. Third, the price: the four-way terms make the circuit deep, 12,716 CNOTs at three layers. And we found no earlier quantum approach that models IM products. D-Wave's frequency example and Vandelli et al. 2025 handle pairwise interference only.

## Slide 9: Where it falls short

- Classically trivial at 16 frequencies, and greedy solves it
- Here it is a Golomb-ruler search, a link known since 1953 (Babcock)
- Third-order only: no fifth order, TV data, power or distance
- Angles tuned by exact simulation, which does not scale
- 12,716 CNOTs at p = 3, and not run on hardware
- 21: grid points for 6 mics, with just 2 clean plans
- 782+: qubits for 40 mics on this grid, with 39.6 million 4-qubit terms

**Speaker notes.** Where it falls short. At this size every classical method wins. On this grid with exact landings, an IM-free plan is a Golomb ruler with no gap of one, and Babcock linked IM to these rulers in 1953. We only model third-order products, with no fifth-order, no TV channel data and no transmitter power or distance. We tuned the angles with an exact classical simulation, which can't scale. The circuit is deep. Beyond the toy size: six mics need 21 grid points and only two plans work; forty mics on this kind of grid need at least 782 qubits and about 39.6 million four-qubit terms.
